Executive summary

Jarvis already listens (server-side Whisper) and already talks (the phone’s built-in voice), but only one sentence at a time, and it can reach about half of what the app does. This proposal closes both gaps in phases.

The recommendation

Server-side voice, on the GPU we already have

  • Phone streams audio to the server over a WebSocket through the existing HTTPS tunnel.
  • Speech-to-text moves from Whisper tiny to faster-whisper large-v3-turbo on the GPU.
  • Replies are spoken in English by Kokoro-82M (Apache-2.0, commercial use allowed), sentence by sentence.
  • The existing assistant, its tools and its approvals are reused unchanged.

What will not change

Kept as today Nothing is sent without a human tap

Emails, WhatsApp messages and proposals still go through the approvals screen. Voice can prepare them and tell you they are waiting; it cannot press send.

Kept as today Replies stay in English

Jarvis understands English, Kannada and Hindi, including a mix of them in one sentence, and always answers in English — the rule the assistant already follows.

What Jarvis can do today, and how

Every request goes through the same pipeline. Voice today is an input method bolted onto the text box, not a conversation.

How a turn runs

  • Router call on the fast tier (OpenAI gpt-5.4-nano via OpenRouter), then the chosen agent’s tool loop: up to 8 rounds of “model decides → tool runs”.
  • A tool that is unsure raises a question instead of guessing; the app draws it as a card with one-tap answers. Anything outbound becomes a pending approval.
  • The conversation is stored per session, so “send it” or “the second one” resolves against earlier turns.

Voice today

Listening Record a clip, then upload it

The phone records until it hears 2.5 seconds of silence, uploads the whole clip, and the server runs Whisper tiny. No live words appear while speaking. A low-confidence result is shown but not sent. Offline, the phone’s own recogniser is used.

Speaking The phone’s built-in voice

Off by default. Reads the first ~300 characters of a reply with Android’s system text-to-speech. It stops when the mic is tapped; it cannot be interrupted by speaking.

Why tiny is not enough for Kannada

Whisper tiny has a published word error rate of about 100% on Kannada and Hindi Cited [1], and on real Kannada speech in this app its transcripts matched only 6–12% of what was said Measured. That is why the app already refuses to auto-send a low-confidence transcript.

Meeting recording today

Recording runs entirely on the phone: a foreground service records the meeting, and an upload worker sends the file to the server’s meeting pipeline (Whisper large) once it stops. So “Jarvis, start recording” cannot be a server tool alone — the server must tell the app to start its own recorder.

How a spoken turn works

Listening

The phone streams while you talk

Audio leaves the phone in 20 ms Opus frames while you speak, instead of as one clip after you stop. Voice-activity detection ends the turn after about 0.6 s of silence instead of today’s 2.5 s.

Understanding

Transcribe, then run the assistant as today

faster-whisper transcribes in the language spoken. The transcript goes into the same router and agent that typed text uses — so every fix already made to tool choice applies to voice too.

Answering

Say something at once, then the answer

As soon as the router has picked an agent, Jarvis says a short acknowledgement (“Checking your follow-ups”) from a fixed template — no extra model call. The reply is then spoken sentence by sentence, and cards still appear on screen.

Interrupting

Talk over it and it stops

While Jarvis speaks, the phone keeps listening with echo cancelling on. If you start talking, playback stops within a frame and the new turn begins. Speech is cancelled; an action already carried out is not silently undone.

Speech models compared

Checked against primary sources on 14 September 2026. Licence is a hard filter: several of the best-known open voices do not allow commercial use. No model has been benchmarked on our GPU yet — that is the first task of Phase A.

Text-to-speech — English replies

Ruled out by licence

Weights are non-commercial or need a separate contract

  • F5-TTS — CC-BY-NC weights [10]
  • Fish Speech S1-mini (CC-BY-NC-SA) and S2 Pro (research licence) [11]
  • Breeze TTS 2 — highest open Elo (1203), but non-commercial [3]
  • Voxtral TTS and Higgs Audio v3 — non-commercial
  • VibeVoice — authors advise against commercial use

Why Kokoro first

Our reasoning, to be confirmed by listening tests

  • Joint-best independent listener score among the commercially usable models.
  • Smallest by far — leaves the GPU for speech-to-text and meeting transcription.
  • No voice cloning, so no question of whose voice it is.
  • The app already names it as the planned on-device voice, so the same voice can serve offline.
  • To check: how it pronounces Indian names and places, and its slower maintenance pace. Magpie is the fallback.

Speech-to-text — understanding English, Kannada and Hindi

Turn-taking and transport

  • Voice activity detection: Silero VAD (MIT), under 1 ms per 30 ms of audio on one CPU thread Cited [15]. Production voice agents end a turn after 0.5–0.8 s of silence [16][17]; we propose 0.6 s, tuned in testing.
  • Smarter end of turn (later): Pipecat Smart Turn (BSD-2) handles Hindi but not Kannada [18], so it is optional.
  • Interruption: Android’s echo canceller with the voice-communication microphone source; it varies by handset, so playback is ducked while listening on devices without it [19].
  • Transport: WebSocket with Opus at 16–24 kbps [20]. The tunnel supports WebSockets without configuration [21]; WebRTC would be smoother on bad networks but needs a public relay.

Latency budget

The time from the moment you stop speaking to the first word of the answer. Today’s figures come from 47 non-proposal assistant turns recorded between 7 August and 13 September 2026 (medians). Proposal and contract turns are slower — median 9.8 s — because they use the strong model and several rounds.

Where the remaining time can come from

  • Spoken acknowledgement after routing: first sound at about 3 s, with no extra model cost.
  • Stream the final answer from the model instead of waiting for all of it: saves part of the agent time. Estimate 0.5–1.5 s.
  • Skip the router when a short follow-up clearly continues the same agent’s work (“yes”, “the second one”): saves the 1.6 s router call on those turns.
  • A different fast model or provider for routing: a 537-token question taking 1.6 s is mostly network and queueing, which is worth a timed comparison.

What gets installed

On the phone instead?

The owner’s preference is the server, and the numbers agree. On-device speech-to-text options either have no Kannada model or are Whisper tiny again [12]. An on-device voice is free, private and works offline, but a small voice on a mid-range phone sounds worse and drains the battery. The proposal keeps the server as the main path and the phone’s own recogniser and voice as the offline fallback — exactly how dictation already behaves.

LLM budget

Speech-to-text and text-to-speech run on our own hardware and add nothing per minute. The language-model bill is what changes. Costs below are calculated by the app from its own price table for each model, not taken from provider invoices.

The budget guard will switch Jarvis off

Each organisation has a monthly AI budget, $3.00 by default, and the assistant refuses every request once it is spent. Ten users at the expected rate would reach it on about the fourth working day of the month. The budget needs to be set deliberately before voice mode is switched on.

Safety and approvals

Voice makes two things easier to get wrong: acting on a word that was mis-heard, and acting without anyone looking at a screen. The existing gates stay, and one is added.

Reads answer straight away

Nothing changes in the system

“Which follow-ups are due today?” is answered and spoken with no confirmation. A wrong guess costs a repeated question, not a wrong record.

Writes are read back first

New for voice

Every create, edit or schedule is read back — names, dates and figures — and is only carried out after an explicit “yes”. It reuses the existing question mechanism, so the card also appears on screen.

Sends stay a tap

Unchanged

An email, WhatsApp message or proposal can be drafted by voice. Jarvis then says it is waiting in approvals; sending needs the approver’s tap, as it does today.

Doubt means ask, never act

Extends a rule already in the app

A low-confidence transcript is never acted on: Jarvis says what it heard and asks again. Two clients with similar names produce a spoken choice, not a guess.

Rules the confirmation must follow

  • Figures are said as words and digits (“four lakh — 4,00,000”) and dates with the weekday, because “fifteen” and “fifty” are the classic mis-hearing.
  • “Yes” only confirms what was just read back. The same lesson as the recent contract fixes (D46, D50): an agreement reaches the question on screen and nothing else.
  • No confirmation, no action. Silence, “hmm” or a new request cancels the pending write.
  • Recording a meeting keeps its on-screen notice and consent step; Jarvis can open the recorder and start it after a spoken yes.
  • Audio is not kept. Like dictation today, voice audio is transcribed and discarded; the transcript is stored as the conversation already is.
  • No cloned voices. Jarvis speaks with a preset voice; no employee’s voice is recorded to imitate.

Risks

Decisions needed

Replies in English, with English, Kannada and Hindi understood, is already decided.

1 · Approve Phase A, starting with the GPU benchmark

About 2–3 days to measure the shortlisted models on our own GPU and recordings, and roughly 5–8 GB of downloads. Every estimate on this page becomes a measurement, and the final voice and speech-to-text choice is made from it.

2 · Set the monthly AI budget per organisation

The $3.00 default would stop Jarvis within the first week for a team of ten. The expected figure is about $1.80 per user per month; the high case is about $5.

3 · Confirm what voice may do after a spoken “yes”

Proposed: create and edit records, schedule, and start a recording — yes. Send anything, record consent, change configuration or touch money — always a tap on screen.

4 · Infrastructure

Extend the server disk before models are installed. Decide later whether to add a public relay so voice can use WebRTC instead of the WebSocket, which matters mostly on weak mobile networks.

Method and references

How the measured figures were taken

  • Latency and cost per turn: every model call is recorded with its purpose, tokens, cost and duration. Calls were grouped into the assistant turn they served (a routing call plus the agent calls that followed, up to the stored reply) for 119 turns between 7 August and 13 September 2026. Medians and 90th percentiles are over those turns. Turns without a routing call were excluded.
  • Cost: calculated by the app from its price table (last read 22 July 2026), not from invoices.
  • Coverage: the Android app’s API client, the backend routers (169 endpoints) and the six agents’ 41 tools, read on 14 September 2026.
  • Hardware: read from the server on 14 September 2026.
  • Not measured: dictation latency today (not recorded anywhere), and any model on our GPU.

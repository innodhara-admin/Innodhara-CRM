Sales Lead

Your day is a queue of decisions somebody has to make.

A Sales Agent works clients. You work the pipeline and the people on it — approving what the AI drafted, confirming who does what, and unsticking the leads that stopped moving. This is every tab, every screen and every button as a Sales Lead sees them, including the three decisions nobody else in sales can make.

02Getting in the first time

There is no self-signup. Your org admin creates the account and gives you a temporary password.

Sign-in screen

Work email and password

  • A wrong password shows the server's own message rather than a generic one.
  • The server chip at the top names the server this phone points at. Tap it, or long-press the logo, to change it — the backend is self-hosted, so its address is whatever your operator is running.
  • A temporary password gets you exactly one screen, Set your own password: the temporary one, then a new one of at least 8 characters, twice.

Home · bottom

Signing out

Sign out warns you first: it wipes this device's cached copy of your data. Nothing is lost — it lives on the server — but the phone is empty until you sign in again. Your role is re-read on every sign-in, so a role change takes effect the next time you come back.

03An approval, dissected

You will read this card more than any other screen in the app. Every part of it exists to answer one question before you tap: what exactly am I agreeing to?

1
outbound message Decide expires in 6h
2
What you’re deciding Follow-up email to Green Valley School after the site visit on 14 May. They asked for revised timings.
3
Recommendation Send. The client asked a direct question four days ago and nothing has gone back to them.
4
Draft message preview To: principal@greenvalley.edu · Subject: Revised schedule · Channel: email
Dear [Name],
Thank you for making the time on Tuesday. Here are the revised timings we discussed…
5
To change this draft, reject it with a note describing the change and the AI will redraft it.
6
Approve Reject
  1. 1
    What kind, which stage, how long you have

    The first chip is the thing being decided — a message, a proposal, a price change, a plan change or an invoice. The second is the stage: Decide means your answer is final; Recommend means you are advising someone senior. Then a countdown, because approvals expire.

  2. 2
    The facts, in the server's own words

    Not a summary the app wrote — the reason recorded when the approval was raised, shown verbatim. If it is thin, that is genuinely all there was.

  3. 3
    What the AI thinks, kept separate

    The recommendation sits under its own heading, apart from the facts, so you can disagree with the advice without doubting the evidence. It is absent when the AI had no opinion.

  4. 4
    The whole draft, not a preview of it

    For a message approval you get the recipient, the CC list, the subject, the channel and the complete body. You are approving text a client will read, so you are shown all of it.

  5. 5
    There is no edit button, and that is deliberate

    You cannot hand-edit a draft here. Reject it with a note saying what to change, and the AI redrafts. That way the correction is recorded and the model learns from it, instead of the fix living only in one person's typing.

  6. 6
    Two buttons, one of which asks a question

    Approve applies it immediately. Reject opens Why? (goes back to the requester). And if the draft still has fill-in-the-blanks the AI left behind, approving stops first and names them: This draft isn't finished, with Send as it is and Not yet.

04Home — what is waiting on you

Same shape as everyone's Home — briefing on top, a merged feed under it, day cards at the bottom — but your feed carries a card nobody else's does.

Top of Home

Your briefing

A written summary of the day, stamped updated 20m ago. If the server is unreachable the last briefing stays put with the reason underneath rather than being blanked. The bell, top right, carries your unread count; pull down to refresh everything at once.

Middle of Home

Waiting on you

  • Task assignments proposed — 7 task assignment(s) proposed. This card only appears for you, an operations lead or the admin. Covered in full below.
  • Approvals, with Approve and Reject inline so the simple ones never need a second screen.
  • Meeting needs your review — extracted items awaiting a human.
  • Newly opened leads — an agent just opened one; anyone on the floor might end up visiting.
  • Inbound messages need a human — the triage count, which taps through to the Inbox.
Empty is an answer

You're all caught up. — not a loading state.

Bottom of Home

Demos, follow-ups, and the way out

Two cards summarise the rest of the day and open full screens: Demos (3 today, or 12 this month) and Follow-ups (4 due today or none due today). Offline they read unavailable offline instead of showing a stale number. Sign out is at the very bottom.

05The Approvals inbox

Tab 4

The queue

Every approval waiting, each with its expiry counting down, and Approve / Reject on the row. Tap the row itself for the full card described above.

  • The stage chip opens ticked for you. A Sales Lead lands on Decide stage, because the ones where your answer is final are the ones you came for. Untick it to see the recommend-stage items as well.
  • Type chips narrow to message, proposal, price change, plan change or invoice. One at a time.
  • Nothing waiting reads No approvals waiting — When the AI needs your sign-off, it lands here.

Tab 4 · after you decide

What happens next

  • A decision applies straight away and the row clears from every screen it was on, including Home.
  • Offline, it says Queued — will apply when you're back online and applies itself when the signal returns.
  • If the server refuses on the way through — an expired approval, someone else got there first — the card shows the refusal with a Dismiss. It is never silently dropped.
Rejecting is not a dead end

The note you write goes back to whoever raised it, and for an AI draft it goes back to the AI, which redrafts against your words. Rejecting with too long, and drop the pricing paragraph is faster than editing.

06Confirming the assignment batch

When tasks come out of a meeting, the AI matches people to them and stops. Nobody is notified, and no work starts, until a lead confirms the batch. That lead is you.

Home card → Confirm assignments

The screen

One row per proposed assignment: the task, the phase it belongs to, and the AI's reason for choosing that person — written out, not scored. At the bottom, Confirm 7 assignment(s).

  • Change person sits on every row.
  • Confirming reports 7 assignment(s) confirmed and returns you to where you were. Everyone assigned is notified at that moment, not before.

Home card → Confirm assignments

The limit worth knowing

Change person will tell you no

Swapping the person needs the staff directory, and reading that is an admin permission. As a Sales Lead you get: Changing the person needs the staff directory, which your role can't read. Confirm as proposed, or reject via your admin.

In practice: if the AI's picks look right, confirm the batch. If one is wrong, leave the batch alone and ask your org admin, who can change it and confirm. Once confirmed, individual tasks can still be declined and reassigned in the normal way.

07Pipeline — projects and leads

One tab, two lists, a strip at the top to switch. It opens on Projects, because delivery going wrong costs more than a lead moving slowly.

Tab 2 · Projects

Projects

Every delivery project with its status and a health colour — on track, at risk, delayed. Tap one for the full project screen.

Statuses run Planning, Proposal stage, Negotiation, Agreed, Preparation, Delivery, Payment pending, Closed, with On hold and Lost off to the side. Nothing here yet reads Projects appear once a deal is agreed or the Planner proposes one from a meeting.

Tab 2 · Leads

Leads

The same list your agents use: search as you type, Mine and Important toggles, and a chip per state. As a lead you will mostly use the state chips — Cold calling to see what has not been called yet, Cold to see what has gone quiet.

  • New lead is here too, with an owner picker listing your agents. This is how you hand out cold-calling work — the lead is created already assigned.
  • Bulk upload leads takes a JSON file of leads, each optionally naming its owner by email or full name, so a whole list can be shared out in one go. Failures are reported per row with the server's reason.

08Inside a project

A header with status and health, a standing answer to What's blocking?, a row of quick actions, and eight tabs.

Project · header

Header and quick actions

  • What's blocking? — a written answer, refreshable, at the top of every project.
  • Change status moves the project through its statuses.
  • Record meeting here — the consent sheet, then the recorder, against this project's client.
  • Draft message — hands Jarvis the project as context.
  • Catch me up — a written brief on where things stand.
  • Split into branches — for a deal covering several sites. Commercials stay on the parent; each branch gets its own record and delivery plan, invoiced together or separately. Only on a top-level project.

Project · tabs

The eight tabs

  • Overview — next up, and open commitments split into We owe them and They owe us.
  • Plan — every plan version, newest marked active.
  • Tasks — the project's tasks; tap through to act on one.
  • Timeline — everything that has happened, in order.
  • People — stakeholders at the client, their role and influence, and whether they get progress updates. Add stakeholder picks from that client's contacts.
  • Team — who from your organisation is on it. Read-only for you.
  • Conflicts — contradictions the system spotted. Resolve asks What's the call, and why? and records your answer.
  • Reports — Generate this week's client report (queued for approval before it reaches the client) and Generate internal digest. A quiet week honestly produces nothing.
Two things on this screen are not yours

Add member on the Team tab, and Shift phase in the quick actions, belong to operations and the admin. The app tells you so rather than failing on the last tap.

09Leads, as a lead sees them

A lead's detail screen is the same six tabs an agent gets — Overview, Contacts, Projects, Notebook, History, Meetings — with wider powers on the header and one pointed exception.

Any lead · header

You can move any lead

The state chip is a button on every lead in the organisation, not only yours. You run the team and you cover absences, so from Open onward you can move a colleague's lead without taking it off them.

Except out of cold calling

Mark as open and Not interested belong to the lead's owner alone — a Sales Lead cannot overrule them. Only the person who made the calls knows what was said. If the owner has left, reassign the lead to somebody else first and let them answer it.

Any lead · the rest

Everything else on the screen

  • The star marks a lead Important, which is what the Important filter reads.
  • The suggestion banner — AI suggests marking cold — nothing has happened for 21 days — with Accept, Why? and Keep as is. Accepting records "Accepted AI suggestion" in the history; keeping it only hides the banner on this phone.
  • Contacts, card scan, notes, recording and typed notes all work for you exactly as they do for an agent — see the Sales Agent manual for those in full.
  • Proposals open from the Overview tab: change by instruction, upload an edited file, submit for internal review, or Approve & send with the warning This really emails the client from your address.

10Repeat business and handovers

A client who bought once is the warmest lead you will ever have. Two of the three controls that manage that are yours.

A closed lead · Overview

Hand to relationship manager

On any Closed lead — yours or anyone's — Hand to relationship manager sits beside Book demo. It offers two routes: Let the assistant choose, or Choose myself from the list of relationship managers with their designations. Either way the reason is recorded, so Why? can answer it later.

Leaving a client closed is a real choice and stays the default if nobody taps. If nobody holds the relationship-manager role yet, the button says so rather than failing.

A returning client · Overview

Giving out a new round

When a relationship manager notices a client is ready to buy again and accepts the opportunity, every sales lead is told. The client's Overview then shows the rounds of business: Round 2 · They came back to us, marked Needs an owner.

  • Tap it to open Who takes round 2?, pick a sales agent, and Give it to them.
  • Only a Sales Lead or the org admin can do this. An agent sees the round and who owns it, but cannot assign it.
  • A round that came back skips cold calling entirely — that question is already answered.
The other side of this

What the relationship manager actually does between the handover and the round coming back is its own manual: the Relationship Manager.

11Demos — you can book

A lead · Overview → Book demo

Booking one

Book demo appears on any lead past cold calling, and unlike an agent, you can actually use it. Pick a date from the calendar — days where you already have tasks due are dotted, and long-pressing one lists them — an optional time, a demo team, and notes.

  • A scheduling clash comes back as the server wrote it: Heads up: that team already has two demos that day.
  • Then: Booked for 2026-06-04. and The calendar invite is drafted and waiting in Approvals — nothing has been sent yet.

Home · Demos card

The calendar

A month grid with counts per day; tap a day for the list, tap a demo for its detail — client, time, status, team and notes. Reschedule is offered on a scheduled demo, and drafts an updated invite into Approvals.

Marking one completed is not yours

Update status — Completed or Cancelled — retract the invite — belongs to the operations people who ran the demo. You book it; they close it.

12Jarvis, the assistant

Type or dictate. It reads your organisation's real data to answer, and anything client-visible comes back for approval before it moves.

Tab 3

Worth asking as a lead

  • What is due, what is overdue, and which follow-ups are waiting — across the team, not just you.
  • A brief on any project, and the details of any client.
  • Why? — explain a decision. Any AI choice on any record can be opened up: what it saw, and why it concluded what it did. This is the tool to reach for before overruling something.
  • Search past notes across every client — the institutional memory you cannot hold in your head.
  • Draft and revise proposals; schedule, complete and draft follow-ups; create, close and reassign action items within your team.

Tab 3 · how it behaves

The mechanics

  • The context chip — arrive from a project or a lead and it reads About: Green Valley School, so pronouns work.
  • Recent commands are chips above the box; tap to reuse.
  • The mic dictates and sends. If the server did not hear clearly it fills the box and stops — Didn't catch that clearly — check the text and send it yourself.
  • Approval bars appear inline on anything it drafted, with Approve & send and Reject.
  • New chat makes it forget the conversation. Your leads, tasks and drafts are untouched.
  • It will not invent capabilities. It cannot create a lead, and it cannot do anything your role is not permitted to do — asking gets the same answer as tapping the button.

13Tasks and Inbox, without a tab

Your five slots went to the pipeline and the queue. Both of these screens still exist and still work — they open on top of whatever you were doing, and Back returns you to it.

Reached from a project, a notification or Jarvis

Tasks

A project's Tasks tab, a task notification, or asking Jarvis all land on the same task detail screen: why it was assigned, what it is waiting on, whether it fulfils a client commitment, and its progress.

  • Done is open to anyone — covering for a colleague who finished the work is teamwork.
  • Block / Unblock, New date, Split, Vendor promise and Vendor slipped are yours across the team, not just on your own tasks. That is the difference between your role and an agent's.
  • Decline is the exception: only the person a task is assigned to can decline it.

Reached from Home or a notification

Inbox

The Home card 3 inbound message(s) need a human, or a notification about an inbound message, opens the Inbox with a back arrow.

  • Messages — every outbound message and its status. You land on Whole organisation rather than Mine, because overseeing the outbox is your job. Tap one for its full delivery trace and the exact copy that was sent.
  • Needs a human — attach a message to a client (pick the client, then the contact who wrote it) or to a project, set what it is asking for, read it in full, mark it handled, or Reply — which hands Jarvis a drafted reply that still goes through Approvals.
  • Log a reply at the top records something a client said outside the system — a call, a WhatsApp message — and routes it exactly like an email that arrived on its own.

14What you can and cannot do

The server enforces this; the app only shapes what it offers you. Where something is not yours, you are told who to ask.

15Signal drops, work does not

What still works

You can sign in against the cached session (a banner tells you it is offline), read what was already loaded, and decide. An approval decided offline says Queued — will apply when you're back online and applies itself the moment the signal returns — so a queue cleared on a train is a queue genuinely cleared.

What it will not do

It will not silently drop a queued decision. If the server refuses on replay — the approval expired, someone else answered it — the card shows that refusal with a Dismiss. Counts on Home read unavailable offline rather than showing yesterday's number as today's.

16Four rules that do not bend

Nothing reaches a client without a person

Every message, proposal, report and calendar invite the AI writes waits in Approvals. There is no setting that turns this off.

No work starts until a lead confirms it

The AI matches people to tasks and stops. Nobody is notified, and nothing is assigned, until you confirm the batch.

Every suggestion can be asked why

Who was picked, on what grounds, and what was said that raised a card — recorded, and readable afterwards from Why?.

Closed and lost are different things

Closed means won and delivered. A client who went quiet is Lost for now and can be revived. Mixing them corrupts every report.

17Where this has got to

Live now Four of your five tabs

Home, Pipeline, Jarvis and Approvals are built and running, along with the assignment batch, project screens, demo booking, the follow-up queue and the Inbox.

Not yet The Team tab

Load bars, leave, decline patterns and the skills matrix are designed but not built; the tab says On its way rather than showing an empty screen. Until then, ask Jarvis about workload and use each project's People and Team tabs.

Sales Agent

You own the leads. The app owns the paperwork.

This is the whole of the InnoDhara CRM app as a Sales Agent sees it: five tabs, every screen behind them, every button on those screens, and the short list of things your role is deliberately not allowed to do. Read it once and you will not need to guess at a control again.

01Your five tabs

The bottom bar is built at sign-in from your role. A Sales Agent gets these five, in this order, and they are the only five — switching tabs closes whatever detail screen you had opened on top.

Home

Your briefing, and everything waiting on you.

Leads

Every client you may see, searchable, plus the button that makes new ones.

Jarvis

Ask in plain words. It reads your data and drafts; you approve.

Tasks

Work assigned to you, grouped by how late it is.

Inbox

What was sent to clients, and what arrived that nobody could place.

Only you get an Inbox slot

Of all the roles, the Sales Agent is the only one whose bottom bar carries the Inbox. Everyone else reaches the same screen from a Home card or a notification. That is not an oversight — it is your job more than anyone's.

02Getting in the first time

There is no self-signup. Your organisation's admin creates the account and gives you a temporary password.

Sign-in screen

Work email and password

Sign in with the work email your admin registered. A wrong password shows the server's own message, not a generic one, so read it — it usually says exactly what is wrong.

  • Server chip at the top of the sign-in screen names the server this phone is pointed at. Tap it (or long-press the logo) to change it. The backend is self-hosted, so its address is whatever your operator is running today.
  • Don't have an account? explains that admins create accounts, and offers Copy request template — a ready-made message to send your admin with your email already in it.

First sign-in only

Set your own password

A temporary password gets you exactly one screen: Set your own password. Enter the temporary one, then a new password of at least 8 characters, twice. You cannot reach the app until this is done.

Signing out

Sign out lives at the bottom of Home. It warns you first: signing out wipes this device's cached copy of your data. Nothing is lost — it all lives on the server — but the phone will be empty until you sign in again.

03A lead's life, button by button

Every lead sits in one state, and moving it is always a deliberate tap — the assistant may suggest, never move. This is the whole vocabulary, with the exact control that causes each step. Learn this and most of the app explains itself.

Only the owner opens a lead

While a lead is in Cold calling, only the person it is assigned to can press Mark as open or Not interested — not even a Sales Lead can overrule that. You made the calls, so you answer the question. On a colleague's cold-calling lead the buttons simply are not there.

And from Open onward

You can move any lead you own. On a colleague's lead the state chip will refuse and show the server's reason. If a lead has no owner and you want it, set yourself as owner first — then it is yours to move.

04Home — the morning briefing

Home answers one question: what needs me today? It refreshes every time you come back to it, and pull-to-refresh forces it.

Top of Home

Your briefing

A written summary of your day, generated overnight and stamped updated 20m ago so you always know how fresh it is. If the server cannot be reached, the last briefing stays on screen with the reason underneath rather than being blanked out.

  • The bell, top right, opens the notification centre and carries an unread count.
  • Pull down anywhere on Home to refresh the briefing, the waiting feed and the day cards together.

Middle of Home

Waiting on you

One merged feed of everything blocked on a human decision. Each card can be acted on without leaving the screen, or tapped to open the full detail.

  • Approvals — anything the AI drafted that needs your sign-off, with Approve and Reject right on the card. Rejecting asks for a note, and that note goes back to the AI so it can redraft.
  • Meeting needs your review — a recording finished processing and the extracted tasks are waiting to be checked.
  • Newly opened leads — a colleague just opened a lead. Anyone on the sales floor might be the one to visit, so everyone is told.
  • Inbound messages need a human — the count of messages the AI could not place. Tapping goes to the Inbox triage queue.
When it is empty

You're all caught up. — and that is a real answer, not a loading state.

Bottom of Home

Your day, and the way out

Two cards summarise the rest of the day, and both open full screens described further down: Demos (3 today, or 12 this month when none are today) and Follow-ups (4 due today or none due today). Offline, both read unavailable offline rather than showing a stale number as if it were current. Sign out sits at the very bottom.

05Leads — finding and creating

Tab 2

The list

Every lead your organisation holds that you are allowed to see, newest activity first, each with a coloured dot for its state and how long since anything happened on it.

  • Search at the top filters as you type.
  • Mine narrows to leads you own. Important narrows to the ones anyone has starred.
  • State chips — Cold calling, Open, Active, Cold, Execution in progress, Client execution in progress, Closed, Lost for now. One at a time; tap again to clear.
  • Nothing matching says No leads found and tells you to clear the filters or make one.

Tab 2 · buttons bottom-right

New lead, and bulk upload

New lead opens a short sheet: organisation name (required, at least two characters), then optional city, source, website and notes — plus an owner picker listing the sales agents in your organisation. It starts on Assign to me, which is what most new leads want. Creating one drops you straight into it.

  • Bulk upload leads takes a JSON file: an array of leads, each with name and optionally city, source, website, notes, an owner (an agent's email or full name) and a list of contacts.
  • It uploads one at a time with a running Uploading 4 of 30…, then reports 18 of 20 lead(s) created and lists every failure with the server's reason beside it. Partial success is normal and is not hidden.

06One lead, tab by tab

Open any lead and you get a header — its name, its state chip, its owner, a star for importance and a small timeline — over six tabs. The action buttons at the bottom change with the tab you are on.

Header · every tab

The header

  • The state chip is a button. Tapping it lists every state; picking one moves the lead. The server owns the rules, so a refused move shows its reason word for word.
  • The star marks the lead Important, which is what the Important filter on the list reads.
  • The suggestion banner appears when the AI thinks the state should change: AI suggests marking active — the client confirmed the budget, with Accept, Why? and Keep as is. Accepting moves the lead and records "Accepted AI suggestion" in the history. Keeping it only hides the banner on this phone — it is your judgement, not a change of data, and the app says so.

Tab · Overview

Overview

The facts the team has gathered: address, city, website, source, notes; then open next steps with their due dates, pending follow-ups, and any proposals with their totals — tap a proposal to open it.

  • Book demo is here on any lead past cold calling.
  • On a lead you own that is still Cold calling, this is where Mark as open and Not interested live.
  • On a Closed lead you own, Hand to relationship manager appears beside Book demo.
Book demo will refuse

The button is there for everyone, but booking needs a permission you do not hold. You will get Your role can't book demos — ask your operations lead or an admin to book it for you. It is shown rather than hidden so you know the capability exists and who to ask.

Tab · Contacts

Contacts

The people at this client. Each row shows name, email, phone, their title, and Primary on the main one. A contact the card scanner was unsure about is flagged Needs review until a human saves it.

  • Add contact — name required, everything else optional, plus a Primary contact switch.
  • Scan card — opens the camera with a guide frame and a Torch. Fill the frame with the business card and tap Capture. The image is read on the server and comes back as a pre-filled verify sheet with its confidence and the organisation name printed on the card.
  • Nothing is sent to a scanned contact until you save it. The sheet says so: An intro message will be drafted from what you save — nothing has been queued yet. When you do save, the intro lands in Approvals for someone to approve.

Tab · Projects

Projects

Delivery projects created from this client, with status and health. Tap one to open the full project screen — plan, tasks, timeline, stakeholders, conflicts and weekly reports.

Locked before the cold call

On a Cold calling lead this tab says Waiting on the cold call and names the owner. There is nothing to show until somebody has actually spoken to the client.

Tab · Notebook

Notebook

Everything anyone has learned about this client, each entry stamped with its category, where it came from and how long ago.

  • Add note asks What did you learn about this client? Anything you save here also feeds the assistant's memory of them, so a note written today changes what Jarvis says next week.

Tabs · History and Meetings

History and Meetings

History is every state change this lead has ever had — Open → Active — with the reason typed at the time and how long ago it was. Nothing is ever quietly rewritten.

Meetings lists every recording and typed note against this client, with its length and status; one still waiting on you carries a Review waiting badge. The two buttons at the bottom, Record meeting and Type notes, are the subject of the next section. Like Projects, this tab is locked while the lead is in cold calling.

07Meetings: consent, record, review

A meeting is how work gets into the system. You can record it or type it — both go through exactly the same machinery and produce the same result.

Step 1 · every single time

Consent

Before the recorder opens you confirm the client agreed to being recorded: They agreed verbally, They agreed in writing or Consent captured in app, with an optional note naming who agreed. It is stored against the lead for data-protection purposes.

Asked every time, on purpose

There is no "consent already on file" shortcut, so the app never assumes you have it. One extra tap per meeting is the price of never being wrong about this.

Step 2a · Record meeting

The recorder

  • Tap the mic to start. The status line reads Recording — the screen can be turned off, and it means it: recording continues in the background and a notification brings you back.
  • Pause / Resume and Stop. After stopping you get the length, an optional Meeting title, and then Upload or Discard.
  • Uploads survive a bad signal: the file is kept on the phone and retried, and only deleted once the server has definitely taken it. A refusal shows the server's own message.
  • No microphone permission? Microphone permission was declined — typed notes work just as well.

Step 2b · Type notes

Typing it instead

An optional title, a meeting type — First visit, Negotiation, Kickoff, Review, Closure — and what was discussed, agreed and promised. A sentence or two minimum.

Send to the Planner, and the notes are read exactly like a recording's transcript: tasks extracted, plans updated, follow-ups drafted. When it finishes you are shown what the Planner actually did, and can jump straight to the meeting.

Step 3 · the pipeline

Watching it process

The meeting screen shows six steps: Uploading → Uploaded → Transcribing → Extracting → Review → Done, and polls until it settles. A failure names its reason and offers Retry.

  • When the audio was poor you are told: The transcript confidence was low — double-check anything important against your memory of the meeting.
  • If nothing actionable was found it says so plainly rather than pretending — the transcript is still stored with the meeting.

Step 4 · the gate

Review extracted tasks

Nothing the AI extracted becomes real until you confirm it. Edit or drop anything the AI got wrong — nothing goes live until you confirm. Each row can be kept, edited or dropped, and a row that looks like something already on the books is flagged ⚠ Looks like "Send revised quote" — drop if it's the same. Add an item the AI missed puts your own task into the same batch.

Confirm 6 item(s) makes the kept ones live and cancels the dropped ones. Confirming is also what starts the Planner, so when a project is being built from this meeting the app tells you: the project and its approvals appear shortly after.

08Proposals

You can send proposals — one of the few client-visible things your role is trusted with directly. Open one from a lead's Overview, or ask Jarvis to draft it.

Proposal screen

What you see

  • Commercials — the line items and total, drawn from the pricing table. Machine-generated from the pricing table — words are editable, numbers are not.
  • Narrative — the written part.
  • Revision trail — every change made, the instruction that caused it, and when.
  • View PDF opens exactly what the client would receive.

Proposal screen · buttons

What you can do

  • Change it (by instruction) — say what to change in plain words, e.g. make the tone warmer and shorter. Prices stay locked.
  • Upload edited file — replace the document with your own edited version as a new revision.
  • Submit for internal review — send it round before it goes out.
  • Approve & send — asks for the recipient, optional CC and a note. It warns you first: This really emails the client from your address. Check the recipient.
After it is sent

All four buttons disappear. A sent proposal is a record, not a draft.

09Jarvis, the assistant

Type or speak an instruction. Jarvis reads your real data to answer, and anything it writes for a client comes back to you for approval before it moves.

Tab 3

Asking for something

  • The context chip — arrive here from a lead and the chip reads About: Green Valley School, so "draft them a follow-up" needs no further explanation.
  • Recent commands sit above the input as chips; tap one to reuse it.
  • The mic dictates and sends for you. If the server did not hear clearly it fills the box but stops: Didn't catch that clearly — check the text and send it yourself. It will not put words in your mouth.
  • Speak replies reads answers aloud. New chat makes Jarvis forget the conversation — your leads, tasks and drafts are untouched.

Tab 3 · the replies

What comes back

  • An answer, with a note of which agent handled it.
  • Action cards for each thing it did, with Open to jump to the record.
  • An approval bar whenever it drafted something client-visible: Ready for approval: message with Approve & send and Reject inline. Rejecting asks why, and that reason teaches it.
  • A question, when your command was ambiguous: Nothing has been drafted or sent yet — pick one, or type your answer.
  • Why? on a suggestion opens the decision log — what the AI saw and why it concluded what it did.
Unfinished drafts

If a draft still contains placeholders the AI meant to fill in, approving stops to name them: This draft isn't finished. You can Send as it is, but the client will see them exactly as written.

Tab 3 · scope

What it can and cannot be asked

Jarvis can look things up (your leads, tasks, follow-ups due, demos, approvals, project briefs, past notes, and why a decision was made), write to clients (intro messages, custom emails, follow-up messages), work on proposals (create, price, edit the narrative, prepare the email), manage follow-ups (schedule, complete), and handle action items (create, close, reassign within what your role allows). It can also add a note to a client record.

It cannot create a lead — there is no such capability, and it will say so rather than pretend. It cannot book or cancel a demo for you either: that needs a permission your role does not hold, and asking Jarvis gets you the same answer as tapping the button.

10Tasks

Tab 4

My tasks

Everything assigned to you, in five groups, in this order: Blocked, Overdue, Today, This week, Later. Something you cannot start outranks something you are late on, which outranks today's work. Inside each group the highest priority comes first.

Days are the organisation's days

"Today" means today in your organisation's timezone, not your phone's, so the app and the server never disagree about which day a task is due.

Tap any task

What you can do to one

The detail screen shows why it was assigned to you, what it is waiting on, whether it fulfils a promise made to a client, and its progress.

  • Done — with an optional note. Open to anyone: covering for a colleague who finished the work is teamwork.
  • Decline — only on your own tasks. Pick a reason (Too busy, Wrong skill for this, On-site elsewhere, Other) and the system either reassigns it or tells your phase lead.
  • Block / Unblock — blocking pauses the nudges until you unblock.
  • New date — if the new date breaks something downstream it is routed to your phase lead to confirm instead, and you are told what it broke.
  • Split into two, Vendor promise to record a supplier's committed date, Vendor slipped when they miss it.
Your work, or nobody's

Block, new date, split and the vendor actions appear on tasks assigned to you and on unassigned tasks. On a colleague's task they are hidden — their lead or the admin handles those.

11Inbox

Two tabs and a button. Messages is what went out; Needs a human is what came in that the AI could not place.

Tab 5 · Messages

What was sent

Every outbound message with its status. Filter by status, and switch between Mine and Whole organisation — as an agent you land on Mine, because your own outbox is what you usually want.

  • Tap any message for its delivery trace: what happened to it, step by step, and Open the message that was sent to read the exact copy that left the building.

Tab 5 · Needs a human

Triage

Messages that arrived from an address the system could not match, or that asked something commercial. Tap one for five actions:

  • Attach to a client — pick the client, then pick which contact wrote it. A message finds its client through the person who sent it, so if that client has no contacts yet you are told to add one first.
  • Attach to a project.
  • Set what it is asking for — question, change request, approval, rejection, complaint, payment info, scheduling, acknowledgement. The card shows how it is currently read before you overwrite it.
  • Read the whole message.
  • Mark handled — you dealt with it elsewhere.
  • Reply — once the client is known, this hands Jarvis a ready drafted reply with the recipient already filled in. It still goes to Approvals before it sends.

Tab 5 · top button

Log a reply

A call, a WhatsApp message, a corridor conversation — anything a client said to you outside the system. Log a reply asks who it came from, an optional subject, and what they said (you can dictate it). It then goes through exactly the same understanding and routing as an email that arrived on its own, and tells you how it was read: Understood as: question · Green Valley School. This is how a phone call becomes a follow-up.

12Demos and follow-ups

Both open from the cards at the bottom of Home.

Home · Demos card

The demo calendar

A month grid with the demo count per day; tap a day for its list — client, time, status and which team is running it. Tap a demo for its detail.

Read-only for you

You can see every demo, but rescheduling and status updates belong to operations. Tapping Reschedule tells you: Your role can't update demos — ask your operations team.

Home · Follow-ups card

The follow-up queue

Filter by Due today, Overdue, This week or All pending, with a Mine only switch. Anything past its date is badged Overdue.

  • Mark done — note what happened, and leave Schedule the next one automatically ticked to have the next touch set for you. You are told the date it picked.
  • Draft message (AI) — the assistant writes it; it waits in Approvals like everything else.
  • Open the lead.

13Notifications

The bell on Home carries the unread count. While the app is open it checks for new items every minute and slides a banner in; closed, it checks roughly every quarter of an hour and posts a system notification.

They arrive on separate Android channels — Approvals and Escalations are high priority, Tasks, Reminders and General are quieter — so you can tune them in your phone's settings without silencing the lot. Tapping one marks it read and opens the thing it is about: an approval, a meeting, a lead, a proposal, a project, a task, a follow-up queue, or the triage queue. A daily digest is the one exception — the summary is the notification, and the app says so instead of pretending there is a screen behind it.

14What you can and cannot do

The server is the enforcer; the app only shapes what you are shown. Where a capability is not yours, the app tells you who to ask instead of hiding it.

15Signal drops, work does not

What still works

You can sign in against the cached session (a banner tells you it is offline), read what was already loaded, record a meeting, and approve or reject. A decision made offline says Queued — will apply when you're back online and applies itself the moment the signal returns.

What it will not do

It will not quietly drop a queued action. If the server refuses it when the queue drains, the card shows the refusal — Couldn't apply: this approval has expired — with a Dismiss once you have read it. Day counts on Home read unavailable offline rather than showing yesterday's number.

16Four rules that do not bend

Nothing reaches a client without a person

Every message, proposal and invite the AI writes waits in Approvals. There is no setting that turns this off.

The AI suggests; you move

It can read a meeting and say a lead looks active. Changing the state is always somebody tapping something.

Refusals are shown, not hidden

When the server says no, you get its actual words and who to ask — never a button that silently does nothing.

Closed and lost are different things

Closed means won and delivered. A client who went quiet is Lost for now and can be revived. Mixing them corrupts every report.

17Where this has got to

Live now All five of your tabs

Home, Leads, Jarvis, Tasks and Inbox are built and running, along with every screen described above: card scan, the recorder, the review gate, proposals, the demo calendar and the follow-up queue.

Not yet Push notifications

Notifications are polled rather than pushed today — every minute with the app open, about every fifteen minutes without. Real push arrives in a later update; nothing is missed in the meantime, it just takes a few minutes longer to appear.

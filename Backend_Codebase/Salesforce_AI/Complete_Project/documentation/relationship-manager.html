New role

Closed is not the end of the story

When we finish a piece of work, the client used to fall quiet and stay quiet. A Relationship Manager keeps talking to them, and notices when they are ready to buy again. This is that whole journey, following one client the entire way.

The gap we were filling

A client who bought from us once is the warmest lead we will ever have. But closed was a full stop: nobody owned the relationship afterwards, so nobody noticed when the client was ready for more. We found out when they told us, or we did not find out at all.

The whole loop

Selling used to be a line that ended. With a Relationship Manager it becomes a circle: the teal arc is the part that is new, and it is the only way a client re-enters the pipeline without being cold called again.

What a Relationship Manager can and cannot do

They are not a salesperson. They look after people, not deals — so they can talk to a client and record what was said, but they cannot move a deal along or price anything. The server enforces this; it is not just hidden in the app.

The flow, step by step

Following one client — ABC School — from the end of one sale to the start of the next. Priya sold to them. Meena looks after them afterwards.

Nothing new

The work finishes

The project is delivered and paid, the assistant suggests closed, and a person confirms it. Exactly as it works today.

Example

ABC School’s project is done. Priya marks the lead closed.

The owner, or a sales lead

Hand the client over — or don’t

A closed client gets a new button: Hand to relationship manager. Doing nothing is a perfectly good answer, and stays the default.

Worth saying plainly

We are not pushing every client into relationship management. Only the ones somebody chooses.

The assistant

It picks somebody, and says why

It weighs each manager on three things: whether their areas of expertise match this client, how many clients they already look after, and how many relationships they have handled before. The reason is written down and you can read it.

What the reason looks like

“Meena: expert with kolkata, looking after 3/8 clients, has held 4 engagements before.”

If it picks wrong, choose somebody yourself from the list. Both are recorded.

The relationship manager

Meena keeps in touch

Her app opens on the clients she looks after, worst-neglected first. A client nobody has ever called sits at the very top.

What Meena sees
ABC School is yours to look after Priya handed this client over now that the work is finished.

After every catch-up

The call is written up for her

She records or types the conversation. The assistant writes a short summary, keeps any facts worth remembering, and suggests when to speak next and what to ask about. No task list, no project plan.

Example

“That went well. Suggest a call in about five days. Ask about the new computer lab he mentioned, and the exam season plan.”

A person, never the assistant

Somebody notices they want more

If the client says something a colleague would want to follow up, a card appears with the client’s own words on it. Meena accepts or dismisses it.

Both answers are useful

Dismissing is recorded too — the assistant being wrong is the only signal that its judgement needs adjusting.

Every sales lead

Back into sales, with nobody on it yet

The client returns to open with no owner, and every sales lead is told, with the quote. One of them gives the new round to somebody.

Why no owner

So the round is given deliberately rather than grabbed. Until somebody is chosen, only a sales lead can move the client at all.

Two kinds of meeting

A catch-up and a sales meeting are recorded the same way and treated completely differently afterwards. The interesting half is what a catch-up deliberately does not do.

A sales meeting

Recorded by a salesperson, on a live deal

  • ✓ Pulls out the to-dos that were agreed
  • ✓ Builds or updates the project plan
  • ✓ Suggests moving the deal along
  • ✓ Remembers what was said

A catch-up

Recorded by a relationship manager, on a finished client

  • ✗ No to-do list
  • ✗ No project plan
  • ✗ No nudge to change the deal’s stage
  • ✓ A summary, and the next conversation planned

The card that asks a person

When a catch-up suggests the client is ready to buy again, nothing moves on its own. A card appears carrying the reason and, more importantly, the client’s own words — because somebody is being asked to make a judgement, and a paraphrase is not evidence.

Rounds of business

A client who comes back has two rounds on record, not two lead pages. Contacts, the notebook and everything we know stay in one place — you never re-enter a phone number for round two.

Four rules that do not bend

These hold everywhere in the product, and this role does not get an exception.

The assistant never moves a client by itself

It can notice, suggest and explain. Moving a client between stages is always a person tapping something.

Every suggestion says why

Who was picked and on what grounds, what was said that raised a card — all of it recorded and readable afterwards.

Coming back skips cold calling

Cold calling answers “is there any interest?”. For a client we have delivered for, that question is already answered.

Closed and lost are different things

Closed means won and delivered. A client who goes quiet is lost for now. Mixing them would corrupt every report we produce.

Before it can be used

Somebody has to hold the role

Until an admin creates a user with the Relationship Manager role, the Hand to relationship manager button will refuse and say so — there is nobody to hand the client to. With only one manager the assistant will always pick them and will say as much, rather than pretending it weighed a choice.

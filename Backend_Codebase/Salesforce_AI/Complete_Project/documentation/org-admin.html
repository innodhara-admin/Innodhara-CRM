01Two devices, one organisation

Every other role does their entire job in the app. You do not, and this is the single thing most worth knowing before you start hunting for a screen: the phone runs the business day to day, the browser sets the business up.

InnoDhara CRM · Android

On the phone

Running the day

Decisions, oversight, and stepping in where a role's limits have left something stuck.

  • Approvals — every one raised anywhere in the organisation, at both stages.
  • The assignment batch — confirm it, and change who a task goes to, which no other role can.
  • Any lead, any state — including opening a cold-calling lead whose owner has gone.
  • Any task — block, reschedule, split, vendor actions, right across the organisation.
  • Projects — team membership, phase shifts, conflict resolutions.
  • Demos — book, reschedule, and mark completed or cancelled.
  • Jarvis, with no permission narrowing on anything it can do.
https://<your-server>/dashboard/

In a browser

Setting the business up

The setup console. Same sign-in, same account — a web page rather than an app, because this is desk work.

  • Staff — create accounts, set roles, skills and workload caps, reset passwords.
  • What you sell — programmes, rate cards, proposal templates.
  • Message templates — the introduction and follow-up wording.
  • Demo teams — who runs demos, and their calendar.
  • Integrations — email sending, WhatsApp, calendar, inbound mail.
  • Settings — the behaviour dials the AI reads before it acts.
  • Retention, audit, import and the go-live checklist.
So if you cannot find it in the app, it is in the console

The app's Admin and Insights tabs are honest placeholders — they say On its way and nothing more. Pricing, programmes, templates, staff and settings were never behind them; they live in the console and always have. Nothing is missing, and nothing is hidden. 03Accounts, including everyone else's

Nobody in your organisation can sign up for themselves. Every account exists because you created it.

Console · Staff

Creating a colleague

Name, work email, role, and optionally phone, department, designation, WhatsApp number, working days and a cap on open tasks. Creating the account returns a temporary password — pass it to them, and they will be forced to set their own the first time they sign in.

  • Staff email must use your organisation's own domain. The server refuses anything else and says which domain it expects.
  • Only organisation roles can be assigned here — you cannot mint a platform operator.
  • Forgotten password? Reset on their record issues a new temporary one the same way.
  • Every one of these actions is written to the audit log, with who did it and when.

The app · sign-in

What they will see

Your colleagues sign in with their work email. Someone with no account taps Don't have an account? and is told plainly that admins create accounts, with a Copy request template button that writes you a message with their email already in it. A temporary password gets them exactly one screen, Set your own password, and nothing else until it is done.

Which server?

The backend is self-hosted, so the app carries a server chip on the sign-in screen — tap it, or long-press the logo, to point the phone at a different address. If your operator moves the server, that chip is the answer to "the app stopped working".

04Home — the whole org's queue

Top of Home

Your briefing

A written summary of the day, stamped updated 20m ago so you always know how fresh it is. If the server cannot be reached the last briefing stays on screen with the reason under it. The bell, top right, carries the unread count; pull down to refresh the whole screen.

Middle of Home

Waiting on you

  • Task assignments proposed — the AI's batch, which you can confirm and, uniquely, change.
  • Approvals, with Approve and Reject inline.
  • Meeting needs your review — extracted items awaiting a human.
  • Newly opened leads — announcements from the sales floor.
  • Inbound messages need a human — taps through to the Inbox triage queue, which you have no tab for but can always reach.
Empty is an answer

You're all caught up. — not a loading state.

Bottom of Home

Demos, follow-ups, and the way out

Two cards summarise the rest of the day and open full screens: Demos (3 today, or 12 this month) and Follow-ups (4 due today or none due today). Offline both read unavailable offline rather than showing a stale number as current. Sign out is at the very bottom, and warns that it wipes this device's cached copy of your data.

05The Approvals inbox

Nothing the AI writes reaches a client without a person, and you are the person of last resort for all of it.

Tab 2

The queue

  • Every approval waiting, with its expiry counting down, and Approve / Reject on the row.
  • Both stages by default. A sales lead opens this screen filtered to Decide stage; you do not. You see the recommend-stage items too, which is what oversight means.
  • Type chips narrow to message, proposal, price change, plan change or invoice.

Tap any row

The card itself

The kind and stage, a countdown, the facts recorded when it was raised (in the server's own words), the AI's recommendation under its own heading, and for a message the complete draft — recipient, CC, subject, channel and the full body.

  • There is no edit button, on purpose. To change this draft, reject it with a note describing the change and the AI will redraft it.
  • A draft still carrying placeholders stops you once: This draft isn't finished, naming them, with Send as it is and Not yet.
  • Offline, a decision says Queued — will apply when you're back online. A refusal on replay is shown, never swallowed.
The full anatomy

This card is dissected part by part in the Sales Lead manual — the same screen, the same six parts.

06The assignment batch

When tasks come out of a meeting, the AI matches people to them and stops. Nothing is assigned and nobody is notified until a lead — or you — confirms.

Home card → Confirm assignments

The screen

One row per proposal: the task, its phase, and the AI's reason for choosing that person, written out rather than scored. At the bottom, Confirm 7 assignment(s). Confirming reports 7 assignment(s) confirmed and notifies everyone at that moment.

Home card → Confirm assignments

Change person — the bit only you can use

This works for you and nobody else in sales

Change person lists your active staff with their roles, because reading the staff directory is your permission. A sales lead tapping the same button is told to confirm as proposed or come to you — so when one asks you to fix a batch, this is the screen they mean.

Overridden rows show Overridden → Priya Nair until you confirm. Confirm once and the whole batch, overrides included, goes live together.

07Reaching leads, projects and tasks

You have no Leads tab, no Pipeline tab and no Tasks tab — those slots went to Approvals and the two placeholders. Every one of those screens still exists and you have full rights on all of them; here is how to get there.

Four ways in

Getting to a record

  • Ask Jarvis. The quickest route by far. Ask for a client, a project brief, or what is overdue, and the reply carries Open buttons straight to the record.
  • Tap a notification. Approvals, meetings, leads, proposals, projects, tasks, messages and follow-ups all deep-link to their own screen.
  • Tap a Home card. Newly opened leads, meetings awaiting review and the triage count all open the thing they describe.
  • Follow the chain. A lead's Projects tab reaches its projects; a project's Tasks tab reaches its tasks; a task's dependencies reach each other.

Once you are there

What you can do on each

  • A lead — six tabs (Overview, Contacts, Projects, Notebook, History, Meetings), the state chip on any lead in the organisation, the importance star, contacts and card scan, notes, recording and typed notes, Book demo, and Hand to relationship manager on a closed one.
  • A project — What's blocking?, Change status, eight tabs, and every quick action including the two other roles do not get: Shift phase and Add member on the Team tab.
  • A task — Done, Block / Unblock, New date, Split, Vendor promise and Vendor slipped, on anyone's task anywhere. Only Decline is reserved for the person it is assigned to.
These are documented in full elsewhere

The lead and meeting screens are covered step by step in the Sales Agent manual; projects and the approval card in the Sales Lead manual. Everything they describe, you can do — and more.

08The powers only you hold

Most of the app's rules exist so that people cannot accidentally act outside their job. You are the exception each of those rules is written against — the person who unsticks what nobody else can.

A cold-calling lead

Opening a lead that is not yours

While a lead sits in Cold calling, only its owner may press Mark as open or Not interested — not even a Sales Lead can overrule that, because only the person who made the calls knows what was said. You can.

Use the ordinary fix first

An owner who has left the company is not a reason to reach for this. Reassign the lead to somebody else and let them open it — the answer is then given by a person who has actually spoken to the client. Break-glass is for the case nobody planned for.

Across the app

The rest of the list

  • Change who an assignment goes to — the batch screen, above.
  • Add and remove project team members — the project's Team tab. A sales lead is told to ask you.
  • Shift a project's phase — sales, proposal, preparation, delivery, payment, closure. Yours and the operations lead's.
  • Mark a demo completed or cancelled — and cancelling drafts the retraction of the invite into Approvals.
  • Move any lead through any state, and act on any task anywhere.
  • Accept a relationship manager's opportunity card — you hold that permission alongside them.
  • Erase a person's data, and reset the organisation — both in the console, and both covered in section 14.

09Jarvis, the assistant

With no list tabs of your own, the assistant is not a convenience for you — it is a main route into the app.

Tab 3

What it will answer

  • Leads, clients and their full details; what is due or overdue; follow-ups waiting; demos; approvals outstanding; a written brief on any project.
  • Explain a decision — any AI choice on any record, opened up: what it saw and why it concluded that. The tool to reach for before you overrule anything.
  • Search past notes across every client — the organisational memory nobody can hold in their head.
  • Draft and revise proposals, price them, prepare the email; schedule, complete and draft follow-ups; create, close and reassign action items; book and cancel demos; add a note to a client record.

Tab 3 · the mechanics

How it behaves

  • The context chip — arrive from a record and it reads About: Green Valley School, so pronouns work.
  • Recent commands are chips above the box; tap to reuse.
  • The mic dictates and sends. If the server did not hear clearly it fills the box and stops rather than asking on your behalf.
  • Approval bars appear inline on anything client-visible it drafted. Holding every permission does not exempt you from the gate.
  • New chat makes it forget the conversation; your records are untouched. If the server could not delete the old one, it says so rather than implying it is gone.
  • It will not invent capabilities. There is no create-a-lead tool, and it says so instead of pretending.

10Demos, follow-ups and the Inbox

Home · Demos card

The demo calendar

A month grid with a count per day; tap a day for its demos, tap one for its detail — client, time, status, team and notes. You are the only role that can do all three things to a demo:

  • Book demo from any lead past cold calling — date, optional time, a demo team and notes. Days you already have tasks due are dotted on the calendar. Clashes come back in the server's own words.
  • Reschedule — Rescheduled — the updated invite is drafted and waiting in Approvals.
  • Update status — Completed, or Cancelled — retract the invite, which drafts the retraction into Approvals.

Home · Follow-ups card

The follow-up queue

Filter by Due today, Overdue, This week or All pending, with a Mine only switch you will usually leave off. Mark done takes a note and offers Schedule the next one automatically; Draft message (AI) writes one into Approvals; Open the lead jumps to the client.

Home card → Inbox

The Inbox

You have no Inbox tab, but the Home card 3 inbound message(s) need a human and any inbound-message notification open it with a back arrow. Messages shows every outbound message and its status — you land on Whole organisation, and tapping one gives the full delivery trace plus the exact copy that was sent. Needs a human is the triage queue: attach a message to a client (pick the client, then the contact who wrote it) or to a project, set what it is asking for, read it in full, mark it handled, or Reply — which hands Jarvis a drafted reply that still goes through Approvals. Log a reply at the top records something a client said outside the system and routes it exactly like an email that arrived on its own.

11The setup console, screen by screen

Open /dashboard/ on your server in a browser and sign in with the same account you use on the phone. This is where an organisation is configured, and where almost everything an admin owns actually lives.

Console · the spine

The go-live checklist

The console opens on a checklist rather than a menu, because setting up an organisation has an order: the organisation and its first administrator, email sending, retention windows, calendar, and the suppliers of inbound mail. Each item reports its own state, so you can see what is genuinely done rather than what somebody remembers doing.

Console · Staff

People

  • Create accounts with a role, and hand over the temporary password.
  • Record each person's skills, their working days and a cap on open tasks — the assignment engine reads all three when it proposes who does what.
  • Deactivate someone who has left, and reset a password for someone locked out.

Console · what you sell

Programmes, pricing and templates

  • Programmes — one entry per thing you offer.
  • Pricing — a rate card per programme, versioned. This is why a proposal's numbers are locked in the app: they come from here, and editing prose can never move a price.
  • Proposal templates — one per programme, the shape every generated proposal follows.
  • Message templates — the introduction and follow-up wording the AI starts from.

Console · operations

Demo teams, integrations and mail

  • Demo teams — who runs demos, and the calendar the app's booking screen reads.
  • Integrations — email sending, WhatsApp and calendar credentials. Until email is configured, messages are prepared but have nowhere to go.
  • Inbound mail — the addresses and suppliers that feed the triage queue.
  • Approval rules and playbooks — what needs signing off, and the standard responses the system reuses.

Console · records

Audit, retention and data

  • Audit — who did what, when. Staff created, settings changed, approvals decided. Yours to read, nobody else's.
  • Retention — how long meeting audio and transcripts are kept, in days.
  • Import and bundle — bring a configured organisation in from a file, or export the current one.
  • Reset — covered in section 14, because it is not reversible.

14Three things that cannot be undone

Everything else in this manual is reversible. These are not, so they get their own section rather than a line in a list.

Erasing a person's data

A data-protection request removes an individual's personal data permanently. It is the right thing to do when asked, and there is no recovery afterwards. Confirm the identity before you run it.

Resetting the organisation

The console's reset wipes working data and returns the organisation to a clean state. It exists for trials and rehearsals, not for tidying up. Export a bundle first if there is any chance you will want what is there.

Deleting a programme, rate card or template

Records already generated from them keep what they were built with, but nothing new can be made from a deleted one, and the version history goes with it. Deactivating is usually what you actually want.

And one that only looks irreversible

Sign out wipes this device's cached copy of your data. That is not data loss — everything lives on the server, and signing back in brings it all down again.

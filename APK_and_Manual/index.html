Point the app at your server first

The app talks to a self-hosted backend, so it needs the address before you sign in.

This build ships with an address that no longer works

The server baked into this APK was a temporary tunnel from an earlier demo and is offline. Set the current one below — it takes about ten seconds, and the app remembers it.

https://unaltered-uncommon-bottling.ngrok-free.dev

Checking server…

  1. Open the app. On the sign-in screen, tap the server: chip (or press and hold the logo).
  2. Paste the address above into the box.
  3. Tap Apply, then sign in with the credentials your administrator gave you.

Installing the APK

It is not on the Play Store, so Android asks permission once.

Download it

Tap Download APK above. Your browser may warn that APK files can be harmful — that is the standard notice for any app installed outside the Play Store.

Allow the install

Open the downloaded file. Android will ask to allow installs from your browser or Files app. Grant it, then come back — you only do this once.

Open and point it

Launch Innodhara, set the server address from the section above, and sign in. Your administrator creates accounts and issues the first password.

How it thinks

Four ideas the rest of the product follows from. The manual covers all of them.

The client is the relationship

A client record holds who they are and every conversation. A project is one concrete piece of work — one client can have many, each with its own plan, team and money.

One living plan, versioned

The plan is never edited in place. Every change writes a new version with a plain-language summary, and the old one stays readable forever.

The AI proposes, a person decides

No message reaches a client without somebody tapping Approve, and every AI decision is recorded with its reasoning — so "why did it do that?" always has an answer.

Work is matched by recorded skill

People carry skills on their record. When a plan needs one nobody holds, that becomes an explicit decision for a human rather than a silent fallback.
